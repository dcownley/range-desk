Getting started on a new device

  1. Open the app address in the browser (Chrome on Android, Safari on iPad) and add it to the home screen. Don't open the downloaded index.html file itself: the file previewer doesn't run the app.
  2. In Full view, open Airfield report, then Airfield data relay. Enter the relay address and tap Save.
  3. Enter your passphrase as the Photo reading key.
  4. Place the firing point and target pins on your range, check the load under Edit load, and enter the correct ring sizes under Edit target faces.

Settings, logs and shots are saved on each device and each app address separately. Use Export CSV to keep a record.

At the range: Range mode

The String card at the top runs each string for you. Everything else on the page is there to fill it in.

  1. Before the first sighter, look at the checklist. Green is ready, amber needs a look, red is missing. Tap Set on any line to jump to it.
  2. Tap Start string. The button turns green. Calypso readings are averaged from here until the end of the string.
  3. Shoot. If the light or mirage changes, tap it in Mirage and light; it's time-stamped.
  4. Optionally, tap where the group centred (clicks high/low, left/right). A ShotMarker import later replaces this.
  5. Tap End string and log. One push records the distance, atmosphere, every wind source, your dial (your call), the group and the barrel. If anything is red, it tells you first; tap again to log anyway.
  • Sun mode (top bar) switches to black on white with larger type for bright light.
  • Distance: enter the rangefinder's line-of-sight reading (not AMR). Everything is solved at the true distance.
  • Atmosphere: choose Kestrel / Weatherdot and enter temperature, station pressure and humidity, or use the photo button. Re-enter it every 45 minutes or so.
  • Wind: Calypso (live, Chrome on Android only), or Kestrel / mine (speed and clock position). The forecast model is shown red: it's a starting point, not a measurement.
  • Dial: tap Copy solution, dial it, and correct the figures if you dialled something different. The windage you dial is your wind call.
  • After shooting, import ShotMarker and Garmin under Plotted shots, Import results. Strings are matched by shot timing.

Next shot

The plot sheet's table shows, for every shot, what the App suggests, what was Applied (your call on the scope) and the True value the shot needed, then how many clicks the call and the app were each off. After every shot you plot (tapped from ShotMarker's screen or copied from a paper plot), the Next shot box gives the elevation and windage to put on for the next shot, and the change in clicks. Put on scope sets the dial in one tap.

  • Elevation centres on your recent shots, the latest weighted most. It offers a change only when it comes to a whole click, so one wide shot isn't chased.
  • Windage with the Calypso live: the wind now × this range's trued factor, plus spin drift, plus the string's own recent trend. Once the wind has varied during the string, the factor is refined on the string itself.
  • Without a live wind, it follows where your recent shots needed the wind; watch the flags for a change.
  • Called a poor shot? Tap Last shot: called poor. It still scores, but it is left out of Next shot, the wind bracket, wind truing and Correct from impact, and shows ✗ in the table.
  • Aiming off? Open Point of aim and step it in ½ MOA before the shot; an AIM mark shows on the face. Shots are judged from where you aimed until you choose Centre.
  • Below the reason, Next shot breaks the elevation into its parts: the solver (load, today's air, Coriolis), any truing (today's velocity or BC) and the string's trend.
  • A ShotMarker import afterwards replaces the tapped positions with ShotMarker's, and those strings then feed the trued factor for next time.

Match plan

At the top of the full view. Century is set up already (from Saturday's target 49 and its firing point). For Stickledown, at the range: choose the distance and target, stand at that firing point and tap Pin this firing point (GPS), then do the same at another target on the same mound, well apart. Alternatively, set up any range on the map: under Ranges, choose the range, type a target number, tap Pin it on the map and tap that target's frame on the satellite map; do it for two targets well apart. Century already knows Saturday's target 49. Target numbers always run from 1 on the left to the highest on the right as you look downrange; target 1 is always the left-hand target as you look downrange.

Then add each detail from the squadding: date, time, range, distance, target and position. Range Desk places your target and firing point, takes the forecast for that hour, and shows the opening elevation and windage and the wind bracket (lull to gust), using your trued model factor where you have one. Use loads a detail (distance, target, session and opening dial); range the frame on the day as usual. Print plot sheets gives one sheet per detail with the forecast and opening dial filled in.

Each detail also records the rifle (add yours under Rifles: make and model, serial number) and the load; its forecast and opening dial use that load. The plan shows where the sun will be relative to your line of fire at that time. When you range the frame on a planned detail, the distance is saved when the string ends; later plans use it for that target, and the average for other targets at the same firing point. On the day, True the forecast on live wind (Range mode, Next detail) compares the Calypso or your Kestrel reading with the forecast for this hour and carries the difference into the coming details, fading out over 4 hours; the factor learned from your target plots for that source is then applied. In Range mode, Check my position uses the phone's GPS to confirm the range, distance and target you're standing at.

Records

Records (in the header and in Range mode) lists every string on this device, newest first.

  • Filter by date, shooter, session type, event, distance, load or coach. The summary, the BC and every export follow the filter.
  • Summary: strings, shots, points and V count, average group, average Garmin velocity, and how many clicks of elevation the strings needed against the app's solution.
  • BC from velocity: where a string has both the Garmin's muzzle velocity and ShotMarker's speed at the target, the app works out the G7 and G1 BC that joins the two in that string's air. Use in load puts it into the current load (Undo puts it back); Copy for QuickLOAD gives a short summary to paste into your load notes or QuickTARGET.
  • Batches: set the ammunition batch in Session details whenever you change ammunition. Today's velocity, truing and temperature learning only combine readings from the same batch, and Records shows a By batch table when the strings shown include more than one. If untagged strings fall into two clear velocity groups, Records says so and offers to tag them as two batches.
  • Tap a string to correct its tags (event, detail, shooter, coach, note) or show it on the target.
  • Export the strings or their shots as CSV for a spreadsheet, or save them as a data file to keep. To make room, save first, then Delete these from this device; opening the file again later brings them back.

No signal at the range

  1. At home on Wi-Fi, place the firing point and target pins, then tap Save this range for offline (Line of fire card). It saves the app, the map around the range (about 300 tiles) and the latest weather.
  2. At the range with no signal, open Range Desk as usual. An Offline tag shows at the top. The solver, String card, plot sheet, Calypso and Weatherdot all work; everything you log is kept on the tablet.
  3. The model weather shown is the last saved copy, marked "saved, no signal". Use your Weatherdot / Kestrel and Calypso for the day's values.
  4. Photo reading, airfield reports and the Windy map need a signal. They work again as soon as there is one.

Save each range you shoot at once; save it again before a trip to refresh the weather.

Session details and several shooters

At the top of Range mode, tap Session details once per session: practice, training, coached or competition; the event or range; the match or detail; the coach; and the shooter with their competitor number, rifle and load. Every string, shot and export is tagged with them, together with the date and distance.

  • For a team or a coached group, add each shooter once. Tap a shooter's name to switch; their load comes with them. Switching is locked while a string is open.
  • The checklist's Session line turns red with no shooter, and amber in a competition without the event or competitor number, or in a coached session without the coach.
  • Wind truing uses every shooter's strings at that firing point, because the wind the bullets met is the same whoever fired them.

Plot sheet and wind bracket

While a string is open, the String card shows the target face. Use it as your plot sheet, live or straight after the detail.

  1. Set Sighters (usually 2), To count (10, 15 or 20) and whether you convert the last or both sighters. Range Desk remembers your usual detail.
  2. Before each shot, set Elevation and Windage on the scope to what you had dialled for it. Then tap where the shot landed. "Next" tells you which shot is due.
  3. The table shows, for every shot: what you dialled, what Range Desk said, where it landed, and what it needed.
  4. From four shots on, the Wind bracket shows the lightest and strongest wind the bullets met, the windage for each, and the one setting that would have dropped the fewest points. Use it after the first detail of the morning and the afternoon: start the next detail on the "one setting", come off towards "dropped off" when it eases, and go on towards "picked up" when it builds.
  5. End string logs the detail with its score and bracket, and the wind truing and Correct from impact use every shot with the dial it was fired on.

Cards below the String card fold to a one-line summary: tap a heading to open or close it.

Wind truing

For each string with a windage call and a group, the app works out the effective wind: the crosswind the bullet actually met, from your dial corrected by where the group landed, with spin drift taken out. It compares that with what the Calypso, the forecast model and your own reading said at the time.

  • A factor below 1 means the source reads high. On 31 October the model read high and the Calypso matched the range.
  • Factors are kept per firing point, because shelter and wind shadows differ from range to range.
  • Nothing changes until you tap Use this factor; the wind label then shows "× 0.62 (trued)", for example. Stop using turns it off.
  • Two strings give a first factor; five or more give a steady one.

Correct from impact and truing

When the group lands high or low, enter how many clicks it actually needed in Correct from impact (Range mode), or tap Use the plotted group. The app works out the muzzle velocity that explains the miss and carries it to every distance, so a 3-click miss at 900 yd becomes about 1.6 clicks at 600 and 3.6 at 1,000, not a flat 3.

  • Apply for this session uses the trued velocity for today's solutions without changing your load.
  • Save to load makes it permanent. It needs a second tap to confirm, and Undo last save puts the old value back.
  • Base corrections on group centres, not single shots. Several corrections agreeing give a smaller ± figure.
  • If the Garmin's measured velocity disagrees with the trued value, part of the miss is probably drag (BC), not velocity. Don't save until that's understood.
  • Explain the miss by: Automatic chooses Velocity below 800 yd and Drag (BC) at 800 yd and beyond. For drag, the velocity is held at the Garmin reading for that string (or the velocity already trued at shorter range), and the rest of the miss is put down to BC. Save to load then updates the BC as well, with Undo.
  • Best order on a long-range day: true velocity at 600 or 800 with the Garmin running, then true drag at 900 and 1,000.

Barrel settling

  • In Range mode's Barrel card, choose cleaned or fouled and enter rounds since the last clean.
  • Fire foulers onto the ShotMarker target at the logged dial if allowed; count foulers fired elsewhere with Off-target fouler fired.
  • A round fired more than 10 minutes after the previous one counts as cold bore (you can change this).
  • After the session, tap Save session to barrel history. The forecast table grows with every session.

Updating the app

  1. Download the new index.html (check the name is exactly index.html).
  2. On github.com, open the range-desk repository: Add file, Upload files, drag it in, Commit changes.
  3. After a minute or two, close and reopen the app.

The full setup, the relay instructions and the source documents are in the Reference tab of the Range Desk Development Plan.
